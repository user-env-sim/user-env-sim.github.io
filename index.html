About The Workshop

A large number of applications like A/B testing, user research and usability studies, market research, and recommender systems require real human participants. These studies are slow, costly, hard to reproduce, and often infeasible when the target population is rare, specialized, or difficult to reach. LLM-based digital users and environment simulations offer a controlable, scalable and cost-effective way of studying human-system interaction: simulated users can navigate interfaces, express preferences, follow goals, and interact with websites, apps, tools, and recommender systems inside reproducible sandboxes. However, LLMs are often designed to be helpful assistants, not behave like heterogeneous humans. This leads to challenges in their adoption for user simulations - LLMs can be sytlistically homogeneous, fail to capture the user diversity, and introduce systematic biases.

For LLMs to reliable simulate users, several aspects such as User Modeling, Environment Simulations, Evaluation and Benchmarking, Applications, as well as the Societal Implications must be studied in tandem. The Workshop on Digital Users and Simulated Environments aims to bring researchers and practitioners from Machine Learning, NLP, HCI, recommender systems, and behavioral science to foster interdesciplinary collaboration, research, and discussions.

Themes

Digital User Modeling

How can we build LLM-based digital users that faithfully reflect human goals, preferences, constraints, uncertainty, frustration, and behavioral variation?

Environment Simulation

How can we construct realistic yet controllable sandboxes for different interactive digital environments?

Evaluation and Benchmarking

What metrics, datasets, benchmarks, and protocols are needed to measure simulator quality, such as realism, diversity, controllability, robustness, and fairness?

Grounding in Real Human Behavior

How can clickstreams, interaction logs, surveys, user studies, and other behavioral data be used to improve the fidelity and validity of user simulators?

Responsible Simulation and Societal Implications

What ethical, privacy, fairness, and governance challenges arise when digital users are used as proxies for real human populations?

Applications and Deployment

How can digital users and environment simulation support scalable, reproducible, and cost-effective studies of interactive systems across domains?
